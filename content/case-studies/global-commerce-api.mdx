Global consumer brand Nexa suffered repeated infrastructure crashes during peak flash sales. Legacy relational database locking produced database connection pooling exhaustion and lost sales.

<Callout type="benchmark" title="Throughput Under Stress">
During peak retail surges, the multi-region distributed cart engine processed 180,000 checkout operations per minute with sub-second P99 response times and zero oversold inventory.
</Callout>

## Engineering Bottlenecks

1. **Inventory Locking Contention:** High concurrent requests for identical SKU items caused row-level locks to deadlock Postgres pools.
2. **Global Latency Disparity:** Shoppers in APAC and EMEA experienced 3,000ms+ round trips back to a single US-East central database.
3. **Third-Party Payment Webhook Drop:** Inbound payment notifications from Stripe occasionally timed out during sudden volume spikes.

<TechStack items={["TypeScript", "Node.js", "Stripe", "Redis", "PostgreSQL", "AWS"]} />

## Engineering Solutions

- Multi-region Redis clusters executing distributed Lua scripts for atomic inventory reservations without persistent DB locks.
- Geo-distributed edge caches terminating SSL and rendering dynamic cart states in under 25ms.
- Asynchronous reconciliation workers using idempotent transactional queues to finalize payments reliably.
